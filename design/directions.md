# Gốm Chu Đậu — two directions

## The reading

This is a working pottery studio in Chu Đậu, Thái Tân, Nam Sách, with about thirty
pieces finished and for sale at any time — bowls, plates, vases, tea sets, each one
photographed with its size, its glaze and its price. Nobody buys here: the site's whole
job is that somebody sees a piece, recognises it as the one they want, and rings
0220 3882 345 or opens Zalo about *that* piece. So two kinds of visitor arrive. One
already knows what a celadon glaze is and what a 32 cm vase costs, and wants to compare
the thirty pieces against each other — size, glaze, price — without being told a story.
The other has heard of Chu Đậu blue-and-white, is buying a gift or a tea set, and comes
to be told which piece is good and who made it. The studio sells on the pieces
themselves and on the fact that they are made in this village by these hands — not on
price, not on convenience, and not on range. What it is not: a shop. There is no basket,
no checkout, no stock count, no delivery promise, and every piece is one piece, so a
sold piece leaves the site rather than going out of stock.

The two directions below are those two visitors. They are not two styles.

CHOSEN: Fired in Thái Tân — most people who ring this studio are buying one piece that
matters, and they decide because it is this kiln's; the kiln-dark ground makes the glazes
the only colour on the screen, so a celadon bowl is the light rather than decoration.

## Fired in Thái Tân

- **Suits you if:** most of the people who ring you are buying one piece as something
  that matters — a gift, a tea set, a vase for the house — and they decide because it is
  *yours*, made at your kiln in the village, not because it is the cheapest one they saw.
- **Palette (light):** no — this one is dark, and deliberately: a kiln-dark ground so the
  glazes are the only colour on the screen.
  `--ground #16110E` · `--surface #221A15` · `--ink #F2EBE3` · `--ink-soft #A79687`
  · `--accent #C9521F` (kiln fire) · `--accent-ink #16110E` · `--line rgba(242,235,227,0.14)`
  · `--field-line rgba(242,235,227,0.42)` · `color-scheme: dark`
- **Faces:** display **Bricolage Grotesque** (Google Fonts, OFL) — *this direction's one
  unlock: a grotesk with real character instead of a neutral sans, because the pieces are
  hand-thrown and Inter says nothing about a hand*; body **Inter**.
- **Scale at 1440:** h1 **56px** (leading 1.05, tracking −0.03em), h2 40px, price 28px
  tabular, labels 11px small-caps at 0.18em.
- **First screen:** a full-bleed photograph of the studio running to all four edges, the
  name set over it at the left edge with the village and the hours, and three pieces cut
  out along the bottom band with their price set large under each — picture first, goods
  second, no centring anywhere.
- **The one device:** the piece cut-outs cross the line between photograph and ground,
  overlapping both.

## Thirty on the shelf

- **Suits you if:** the people who ring you already know what they are after — a 26 cm
  plate, a men rạn tea set — and what they want from the site is to see all thirty at
  once, with the size, the glaze and the price, and then ring about number four.
- **Palette (light):** `--ground #FAF8F4` (unfired paper) · `--surface #FFFFFF`
  · `--ink #171A21` · `--ink-soft #5B6171` · `--accent #1B3C8C` (the hoa lam cobalt the
  village has painted with since the 15th century) · `--accent-ink #FFFFFF`
  · `--line rgba(23,26,33,0.12)` · `--field-line rgba(23,26,33,0.45)`
  · `color-scheme: light`
- **Faces:** display **Instrument Serif** (Google Fonts, OFL) — *this direction's one
  unlock: an editorial serif, because blue-and-white Chu Đậu ware is six centuries old
  and the catalogue should read like a catalogue of it*; body **Inter**.
- **Scale at 1440:** h1 **40px** — small on purpose, the screen is carrying six pieces,
  their prices and the phone number — h2 30px, price 22px tabular right-aligned, labels
  10px small-caps at 0.16em.
- **First screen:** no hero photograph; a hairline-ruled catalogue sheet — a four-line
  header across the top with the phone and Zalo buttons, then the pieces as a grid of
  tiles, each picture edge to edge in its tile with name, glaze, size and price set
  under it in rules, running off the right edge of the page.
- **The one device:** the hairline rule system — every piece sits in a ruled cell and the
  rules run past the content to the viewport edge, like a ledger page.
